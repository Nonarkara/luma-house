# MSH pass — the chrome of the plan view

**Date:** 2026-10-27
**Subject:** the Plan view's bottom chrome — the room key under the drawing, the
chrome it sits in, and the assistant field on a phone.
**Method:** the real interface, measured in Chromium at 390 × 844, 1024 × 800 and
1440 × 900. Every number below is measured from the rendered DOM, not read from
the stylesheet, and each was taken before and after the change on the same
machine and the same build pipeline.
**Outcome:** the room key is one row of cells, each as wide as its own room, so
a room's area figure sits with the name it belongs to. The drawing at 1024
grew from 408.61 px to 486.02 px and the chrome under it fell from 35.4 % of
the viewport to 25.75 %. A prompt that is cut on a phone now says so.

---

## Found

### 1. The room key was a grid of cells stretched across the sheet — 1024, and every width below the rail

The key under the drawing was `grid-template-columns: repeat(2, minmax(0, 1fr))`
at ≤1100 px: two cells of **448 px** each, three rows deep, **134 px** tall.

Each cell held a number, a name and an area figure, laid out as
`20px minmax(0, 1fr) auto` — the name in a column that takes all the leftover
width, the figure pushed to the far edge of the cell. Measured on the rendered
page at 1024 × 800:

```
1 Quiet bedroom          9.8 m² | 2 Tea + study niche     5.3 m²
3 Dry / wet bath         6.8 m² | 4 South living room   12.6 m²
5 Kitchen + dining       9.4 m² | 6 Entry + laundry      6.1 m²
```

The gap between a room's **name** and its **own area figure** was **290 px** of
blank cell. The distance from that figure to the **next room's name** was
**73 px**. The figure belonged to the wrong room: it was nearly four times
nearer to its neighbour than to the name it answered for. The 1440 px view did
not have this defect — there the key is a 240 px rail beside the drawing and
the same rule reads as an index — which is why the defect only appeared below
1100 px.

**The cost.** 134 px of band under a 408.61 px drawing. The chrome below the
drawing was **283.39 px — 35.4 % of an 800 px viewport** — and the drawing,
which is the work, was the smaller of the two.

### 2. The phone cut the assistant's own prompt mid-word, and said nothing — 390

The assistant field is 205.64 px wide on a phone. Its prompt — *"Quick action:
brighter, budget, photo…"* — needs about 330 px at the 16 px the field must
use (any smaller and iOS Safari zooms the viewport on focus). The rendered
prompt read:

```
Quick action: brighter, budg
```

No ellipsis, no second line: a fragment, cut by the field itself. The studio's
own rule — *a cut must say so* — is one of the five the layout is gated by, and
this cut said nothing.

### 3. The drawing did not outweigh the chrome it sat in — 1024

At 1024 the chrome under the drawing was 283.39 px and the drawing 408.61 px.
The 3D view's own gate says the model must outweigh the chrome around it and
that the footer stays under a third of the viewport. The plan view had no such
gate, and at 1024 it failed both.

## Changed

### The room key is one row of cells, each as wide as its own room

Below 1100 px the key is now a single row of cells — the treatment the phone
already had — with each cell as wide as the room it names:

```
1 Quiet bedroom    2 Tea + study niche   3 Dry / wet bath   4 South living room …
  9.8 m²             5.3 m²                6.8 m²              12.6 m²
```

* **The figure is with its name.** The area sits 4 px under the name it
  belongs to, in the same cell, at every width.
* **The row scrolls when the rooms do not fit**, and it says so: the accent
  scrollbar, the snap, and a cell cut at the edge as the cue. The numbers stay
  on every cell, so the row still keys the drawing.
* **The cells fill the width they are given** and never shrink below their own
  content, so a name is never wrapped to fit a cell that had no room.
* **The wide rail is unchanged** (≥1100 px): a 240 px numbered table beside
  the drawing, which is where the key reads best.

### The assistant field says so when it cuts its prompt

One line of CSS: `text-overflow: ellipsis` on the field. The phone now reads
*"Quick action: brighter, bu…"* — the cut announces itself, which is the form
the law allows.

### The gate grew to own both

`e2e/msh-chrome.spec.ts` now holds **fifteen rules** (the twelve that own the
3D view, and three that own this one):

* *the plan drawing outweighs the chrome below it* — the drawing is taller than
  everything under it, and that chrome stays under a third of the viewport, at
  1440, 1024 and 390.
* *the room key is one row, and every figure belongs to its own room* — the key
  under the drawing is one row, and for every room the distance from its name
  to its own figure is not greater than the distance from that figure to the
  next room's name.
* *the assistant prompt is never cut without saying so* — the prompt is measured
  against the field it is drawn in, and if it does not fit it must be ellipsised.

## Verified

Measured on the same machine, before and after. The "before" column was taken
from the deployed site at commit `eb61857`; the "after" column from this build,
on the same host, with the same measuring script:

| viewport | the drawing before → after | the chrome below it before → after | the key's band before → after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1440 × 900 | 642.61 → **642.61 px** | 149.39 → **149.39 px** (16.6 %) | a 240 px rail → **a 240 px rail** |
| 1024 × 800 | 408.61 → **486.02 px** | 283.39 → **205.98 px** (35.42 % → **25.75 %**) | 134 px, 3 rows → **56.59 px, 1 row** |
| 390 × 844 | 423.41 → **423.41 px** | 267.59 → **267.59 px** (31.71 %) | 56.59 → **56.59 px** (a scroller) |

* **The building itself grew at 1024**, because the drawing's viewport grew:
  the drawn apartment measured **551.39 × 359.49 px** before and **655.59 ×
  427.51 px** after — **+18.9 % on each side, +40.8 % in area** — and the zoom
  fell from 186 % to 180 %. The sheet was not scaled down to buy the chrome
  back.
* **The relationship is measured, not asserted.** Before the change, at 1024,
  the distance from a room's name to its own figure was 290 px and the distance
  from that figure to the next room's name was 73 px. After the change the
  figure is under its own name, 4 px below it and 0 px to the side.
* **The drawing outweighs the chrome below it** at all three widths:
  642.61 vs 149.39, 486.02 vs 205.98, 423.41 vs 267.59.
* **The key under the drawing is one row** at 1024 and 390, and the 1440 rail
  is untouched.
* **Nothing in the key is wider than the window it is drawn in**, and the strip
  is a declared scroller: at 1024 the six cells fit in 896 px and the row does
  not scroll at all.
* **The whole suite.** Lint clean, `tsc -b` clean, **631 unit tests**, **54
  worker tests**, and the browser suite green on CI.
* **The mutation check.** Fifteen rules, fifteen mutations, each killed by the
  gate that owns it, the tree restored afterwards. The three new mutations put
  the historical defect back — the key as a stretched two-column grid, the
  chrome fattened by 200 px, and the prompt cut with the ellipsis removed — and
  each one fails the gate that owns it.

## Remaining

* **The phone still gives a third of its screen to chrome: 267.59 px of 844
  (31.7 %).** The drawing is 423.41 px, so the work outweighs its frame, but
  the tool palette (89 px) and the coach (45 px) are most of what is left. The
  tool palette is drawing chrome, and the coach is a mandated feature; neither
  was moved here.
* **The key scrolls on a narrow tablet.** Between 721 and ~840 px the six cells
  of the sample plan need about 780 px and the row is 672–692 px wide, so the
  strip scrolls and the cell at the edge is cut by the scroller. That is the
  deliberate scroller's cue, and the cells snap to whole cells when scrolled —
  but a name can still be sliced at the very edge. The alternative — shrinking
  the cells and ellipsising the names — was measured and not chosen: it turns
  a name the reader can see in full into a fragment, which the law treats
  worse than a scroller.
* **The assistant bar (45–48 px) is in every view.** It is the coach and the
  journey's next step. Kept deliberately, not by accident.
* **The zoom control stays outside the drawing.** It could move onto the sheet
  at 1024 and take ~46 px, but the law keeps controls outside the physical
  drawing viewport. Not done.
* **The field-dimension annotations still hang outside the drawing** (recorded
  in `docs/SHIPPABILITY-AUDIT-2026-10-23.md` §10.1). Unchanged by this pass.
* **The 3D section-cut control keeps a 214 px footprint**, and the 3D view can
  take a long time to mount on a weak machine. Both are recorded in
  `docs/MSH-PASS-2026-10-25.md` and neither is changed here.

## Appendix — how to reproduce the numbers

```
npm run build && npx vite preview --port 4186 --strictPort
```

Open `/#plan=<the sample apartment's hash>` at 390 × 844, 1024 × 800 and
1440 × 900, stay in **Plan**, and read the bounding boxes of `.plan-viewport`
and `.plan-room-key`, and the rectangles of every `.room-name` and
`.room-area` inside the key. `e2e/msh-chrome.spec.ts` does exactly this and
fails if the numbers move.

**The evidence.** `docs/evidence/msh-2026-10-27/` holds the captures of this
pass at the three widths, with the measured numbers beside each one.

**The mutation check.** Each rule was broken on purpose, the bundle was
rebuilt, and the gate that owns the rule was run; a mutation that survives
means the rule is not owned. The three new ones, with the anchors they change:

| mutation | file | what it restores | the rule that must catch it |
|---|---|---|---|
| the room key is a stretched grid again | `src/design/studio.css` | the key becomes `display: grid` with `repeat(2, minmax(0, 1fr))` and the cell becomes `16px minmax(0, 1fr) auto` — the historical defect | *the room key is one row, and every figure belongs to its own room* |
| the plan chrome fattens again | `src/design/studio.css` | `padding-bottom: 200px` on `.canvas-frame.is-plan` | *the plan drawing outweighs the chrome below it* |
| the assistant prompt is cut without saying so | `src/styles.css` | `text-overflow: ellipsis` is removed from the assistant field | *the assistant prompt is never cut without saying so* |

The twelve mutations that own the 3D view are listed in
`docs/MSH-PASS-2026-10-25.md`; the harness parses the verdict out of
Playwright's JSON report, so a dead preview server, a timed-out navigation, a
browser that never launched or a trace that could not be written is reported
as an infrastructure failure and retried, never as a kill.
